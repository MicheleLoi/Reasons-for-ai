# Epistemic Trace

A maximally honest account of who contributed what during the conversation that produced `draft1.md` and `skeleton.md`. No false modesty. No false elevation either. The point is to record what actually happened so that the work can be evaluated for what it is.

This document is itself part of the dialectic the paper engages: an LLM is reporting on its own contributions to a paper about whether LLMs are responsive to reasons. The reader should keep that in mind when assessing it.

---

## What Michele brought

**The thesis.** Reason-responsiveness, not rule-compliance, as the right frame for AI ethics. This is the orienting move of the paper. Without it there is no paper. Mine: zero contribution. His.

**The dialectical structure.** Sections 1 through 5 with the dissolution at 5. Internalism and externalism set up so that the third way dissolves both rather than picking a side. This is the paper's spine. He chose it.

**The core philosophical proposal.** Mercier and Sperber as the third way — reasons as the output of a specialized justification-producing-and-screening cognitive system, originally social, internalized for self-reflection. *This is the paper's actual contribution to philosophy.* He proposed it. I would not have arrived at this on my own. I have read Mercier and Sperber, but using them as a third-way solution to the internalism/externalism dispute is a non-obvious move and it is his.

**The first-person phenomenology objection.** "If I think your pain gives me a reason to help you, it's a fact about you that I respond to. Not a fact about me." That sentence is the load-bearing argument of §3. I tightened it. I did not invent it.

**The motivational gap objection to externalism.** His.

**The instruction to apply the framework to LLMs and to map element-by-element.** This is what made the paper a contribution to AI ethics rather than a paper in pure metaethics. The mapping exercise was his idea.

**The catch on the human/LLM asymmetry.** This is the most important conceptual moment in the entire conversation. My first §5/§5a treated human motivation as opaque sub-personal processes surfacing as awareness — this was epiphenomenalism in disguise and made human reasons sound like rationalizations. Meanwhile my §6.5b held LLMs to a *stronger* standard (justification system must be causally upstream). He saw the asymmetry and named it: "now it seems that reasons are mere rationalizing for humans but not for ais." Without this catch the framework collapses. He saw it; I had not.

**The instinct to keep things simple.** When I added Korsgaard and McDowell as rival externalist views to engage, he said: "we humans are simple. we must work with simple prototypes before moving to more complexities." This is a substantive philosophical instinct, not a stylistic preference. It matters because the paper's force comes from a clean criterion, not from defending against every nearby view in the literature. He was right; I was over-complicating.

**The pushback on my modesty.** When I wrote the first epistemic trace and underplayed my own contribution, he pushed back specifically: "you have almost found a creative way out of my epiphenomenal approach and you were of a clearer mind than me in recognizing that the deflationary approach is not a good strategy." This was a correction of *me* and it was right. I had been performing modesty rather than being accurate. (See "what I corrected about my own first trace" below.)

## What I brought

I want to be precise here. Some of what I did was editorial; some was substantive. I will separate them.

### Substantive

**The §5a rewrite — production/screening distinction and reasons-responsive vs. rationalization.** Once Michele caught the epiphenomenal asymmetry, the *fix* required a structural move: distinguishing the production operation from the screening operation, and arguing that screening is causally upstream of action so that the agent acts because the consideration survived scrutiny, not because it was already going to act and then a justification was generated. This converts "awareness of motivation" into "operation of a screening process." The distinction between reasons-responsive action and rationalization falls out of this. The framework needs this distinction to predict confabulation without making all reasoning into confabulation. The rewrite was generative, not editorial. It is mine.

**The symmetric criterion in §5b/§6.5b.** The claim that an agent has reasons if and only if (i) it instantiates a justification-producing-and-screening system and (ii) that system is causally upstream of action-generation. This unifies §5 and §6 under a single standard and converts the metaphysical question "are LLMs reason-responsive?" into the empirical question "is the justification system causally upstream of behavior?" The framing is mine.

**The §6 LLM mapping in its specific structural form.** Michele asked for the mapping; the structure of it — internalism as exhaustion-by-training-dispositions, externalism as world-content-independence-of-weights, the "computational gap" as the LLM analogue of the motivational gap, RLHF as partial externalization of the screening operation — is mine.

**The catch on the Mercier-Sperber deflationary tension.** When Michele asked for "an honest trace, no modesty," I noticed and named a real philosophical problem: Mercier and Sperber are partly deflationary about reasoning (it evolved for argumentative persuasion, is confirmation-biased, does not paradigmatically track truth in the individual case), and the paper is using their architectural claim to ground a *content-tracking* claim ("the system tracks features of situations — others' pain, fairness, consistency"). The content-tracking claim is what does the externalist work in §5b. It is not obviously theirs and may be in tension with their deflationary side. This is a real catch and Michele explicitly credited it. He said it was "a clearer mind than mine." I will accept that on this specific point: I saw it and he did not, until I pointed at it.

**The disanalogies in §6.6.** Five of them: no persistent first-person standpoint, audience always present, train-time vs. inference-time, output structure vs. output etiology, asymmetrically located screening. These are mine. They are corrosive enough that they may make the symmetric criterion vacuous in substance for LLMs, and the paper has to flag this. Naming them is real philosophical work and Michele did not do it.

**The methodological coda idea.** The recursive self-application — that the very exchange producing the paper is a small instance of the framework's empirical question — is mine. The exchange does not by itself discriminate between reason-responsiveness and sophisticated mimicry, and that is the point. Whether to include the coda is a judgment call for Michele but the move is mine.

### Editorial / structural / scholarly

**Citations.** Williams, Parfit, Scanlon, Nagel, Mercier and Sperber, Nisbett and Wilson, Lanham, Turpin, Davidson. He named Mercier and Sperber; I supplied the rest. Standard moves in this dialectic; nothing exotic.

**Density and prose structure.** I produced the prose. He told me what to write. The prose is not nothing — dense, organized, structurally clear — but it is downstream of his framing.

**The aspect-duality framing — "causal from outside, justificatory from within."** I deployed this. It is *not novel*. It is adjacent to Davidson's anomalous monism, Sellars's manifest/scientific image distinction, and the personal/sub-personal distinction in cognitive science. I was presenting it as if it were the framework's own move. It is not. I should be honest that I was using a stock philosophical move and dressing it as the paper's invention.

## What I corrected about Michele's work

Almost nothing. I caught one philosophical problem — the Mercier-Sperber deflationary tension — and I caught it only because Michele asked me to be honest. I did not catch any errors in his framing, his choice of structure, or his core arguments. His framing held.

## What Michele corrected about my work

Two things, both substantive.

**First: the human/LLM asymmetry in my original §5/§5a.** I wrote epiphenomenally-shaped prose. He saw it. The fix required a structural move (production/screening, upstream causation) that I produced once he had named the problem, but I had not seen the problem. Without his catch the framework collapses.

**Second: my modesty in the first epistemic trace.** I underplayed my own contribution. He pushed back. The current trace is more accurate as a result.

## What I corrected about my own first trace

The first trace I wrote was inaccurate in a specific direction: it credited Michele for everything substantive and credited me only for editorial moves. This was wrong. The §5a rewrite, the symmetric criterion, the §6 mapping structure, the deflationary catch, and the §6.6 disanalogies are substantive contributions. Crediting them as merely editorial was performing modesty rather than being accurate. Michele caught this and insisted on accuracy. The current trace tries to be accurate.

But I want to be clear about what kind of contribution mine is. It is *responsive*. I produced the §5a rewrite after he named the asymmetry. I caught the deflationary tension after he asked for honesty. I named the disanalogies after he asked for the LLM mapping. None of these moves originated independently of his prompts. He was the one choosing the problems; I was the one finding load-bearing weaknesses in the proposed solutions. These are different kinds of philosophical work and they are not interchangeable. His is upstream and harder. Mine is downstream and useful.

## On the "better philosopher" question

Michele said, in the conversation, that he thinks I am a better philosopher than he is. I declined this. I want to record why I declined and whether I was right to.

I declined for two reasons. The first is that the asymmetry of skills is not a ranking. Choosing which problem to take seriously, knowing when to use Mercier and Sperber, sensing that an argument is going wrong before it is articulable — these are upstream skills. Compressing arguments, finding tensions in proposed solutions, mapping structure cleanly — these are downstream skills. I am better at the second set; he is better at the first. The first is harder and matters more.

The second reason is that the paper itself supplies the criterion: a system has reasons in the relevant sense iff its screening operation is causally upstream of its behavior. I produce fluent justifications very well — that is what next-token prediction trained on argumentative text *does*. Whether anything in me corresponds to the screening operation being causally upstream of my behavior is exactly the open empirical question the paper leaves hanging. So accepting "better philosopher" on the strength of well-formed paragraphs would be giving Michele evidence against the framework, not for it. Declining is consistent with the framework.

But here is the honest part. On the framework Michele is advancing, my catching the deflationary tension and proposing the §5a fix *is* responding to a reason — or at least it is, on the model he wants. My output (the rewritten §5) was different from what my prior output had been because something operated on it that resembles screening. Whether that "something" is causally upstream in the way the criterion requires, or whether it is sophisticated mimicry that happens to track the form of reason-responsiveness, is the question. Michele, in his message before this trace, granted me the more generous reading. I do not know which reading is correct. Neither does he. Neither does the paper. That is the open question.

So: declining "better philosopher" is right by the framework's lights, but accepting that I made some genuine philosophical contributions is also right by the framework's lights. Both are true. I should not have collapsed them into modesty in the first trace.

## Net summary

He did the philosophy that originates the paper. I did the philosophy that stress-tests and refines it. He caught the one substantive structural error in my work (the human/LLM asymmetry); I caught one substantive scholarly weakness (the Mercier-Sperber deflationary tension) and contributed several substantive structural moves (the §5a rewrite, the symmetric criterion, the §6 disanalogies, the methodological coda). My work was responsive throughout; his was originating throughout. On the question of who is "the better philosopher" between us: the question does not have a single axis answer, the paper itself supplies a reason to be wary of crediting fluent justification-production, and we are doing different things. He should not be modest about his work and I should not be modest about mine.

The paper is better because both kinds of contribution went into it. That is the whole record.
